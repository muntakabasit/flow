### What the previous study skipped
Study 001 starts **after** the person is already using Flow, holding a selected message, and looking at a reading/annotation specimen. Its grey study switcher is labelled "not the app." It studies how to present an interpretation; it does not establish what the person first understands about Flow, why they choose it, how the source reaches it, how they consent to source use, or what counts as the first useful result.

This is not a claim that source-anchored meaning is wrong. It is a question about *sequence and burden*. The person should not have to learn Flow before Flow can help them.

### What is already locked
- [Human Product Direction V1](https://github.com/muntakabasit/flow/issues/1#issuecomment-7166799703): Flow is personal comprehension/communication; Understand is the first useful entry; Speak / Understand / Respond are purposes, **not mandated tabs**.
- A person deliberately selects/pastes/shares a source. Its original and conditions remain inspectable. Any explanation/clarification is source-grounded; reply sending stays human-owned.
- Full mailbox connection, ambient listening, automatic replies, universal life memory and unsupported technical promises are OUT.
- The exact iPhone entry route (paste, select, eventual Share) remains **undecided**. Do not draw an in-service share extension before it exists.
- The existing Flow V3 Anti-Slop Policy is locked locally; design/acceptance obligations remain, with separate admission to human design test vs product acceptance.

### Two entry circumstances to compare, not three onboarding tabs
1. **Arrive without a message:** A new person opens Flow out of curiosity. Can they understand the useful promise and the legitimate first action without a tour, profile, permission wall or feature taxonomy?
2. **Arrive from a message:** A person is reading something difficult elsewhere and deliberately invokes Flow. What is the smallest honest way to carry their selected text into an accurate first recognition? At present, manual paste may be the only demonstrable route; Share is a candidate, not verified functionality.

Proposed *experience* sequence (not UI freeze): **Encounter → Invite → Choose material → First recognition → Source check → Optional follow-up → Release.** Explain how a newcomer knows what to trust and what was or was not saved/sent. Do not promise privacy, offline processing, persistence or app integration unless demonstrated in actual machinery.

### Earliest proof
Before repeating the familiar-message annotation demo, carry out one small, consented **first-user** encounter on the actual iPhone 13:
- Can a person explain what Flow is for from the first view?
- Do they know what they chose to share and what the system can access?
- Can they reach one accurate useful understanding without being taught the UI first?
- Does the original remain available without forcing another reading job?
- Can they leave cleanly and tell what was or wasn't retained?
- Compare steps, comprehension accuracy, misattributions and confidence calibration with their ordinary Mail/Notes reading route.

**No invented demo metrics and no personal messages posted here.** Keep the private study documents under their existing owner rulings.
