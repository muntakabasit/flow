### 1. Product recommendation: Navigate should be a *consequence of understanding*, not a fourth destination

The user arrives with a situation, not a software taxonomy. For the locked V1 selected-message encounter, **Speak / Understand / Respond / Navigate** may be useful internal organising verbs, but four equal tabs, four cards or four agents would turn the human's problem into interface administration. The first question should be visible in context: **“What does this mean for me, and what can I do next?”** Do not automatically expose all four actions for all sources.

A proposed human-facing sequence is:

**Original message (intact) → short faithful meaning → material condition/deadline/uncertainty → optional “What can I do next?” → sourced legitimate route → human-owned choice → return to original.**

This is progressive disclosure, not an enforced wizard. A person who only wants translation should be able to stop after translation. A person who needs to act should not have to restart in a separate Navigate mode.

### 2. The crucial design distinction: a next step is not an action button

Avoid a persuasive primary button such as **“Cancel subscription”** or **“Apply now”** when Flow has only explained a document. Instead, distinguish:
- **Source says** — precise quoted/supported condition and original passage, preserving negation, dates, exceptions and jurisdiction.
- **Flow explains** — plain-language interpretation, explicitly separate from source and official guidance.
- **Possible next step** — a suggested user-controlled route, with prerequisites and uncertainties.
- **Official destination** — a verified, applicable link or a truthful “not verified”; never a fabricated link or one inferred solely from source-page instructions.
- **Completed** — only if actual completion has been independently evidenced by the responsible system, never from a generated reply or an opened link.

Use everyday wording in the UI; the distinctions need not appear as five bureaucratic headings. Make essential conditions prominent without flattening legal/administrative complexity. A confident tone must never visually disguise uncertain evidence.

### 3. A mobile-first encounter should feel like a calm instrument, not another AI dashboard

**One focus at a time.** The message remains visible and recoverable. Show a concise meaning, then a small contextual opening into next steps. Avoid stacked action cards, repeated “Ask AI” buttons, an ever-present chat composer, and decorative urgency badges. Preserve real text selection, readable line length, large accessible tap targets, text scaling, screen-reader labels, keyboard and voice-independent operation.

**Offer depth, not compulsory complexity.** Allow “Show original”, “Why does it say that?”, “What do I need?”, and “Help me write a reply” where appropriate. Don't hide exceptions behind an attractive summary. Support a clear exit and return to the exact passage/question. A user should never have to remember which tab or worker holds the source.

**Voice is optional and honest.** In an explicitly initiated session, acoustic cues can indicate listening, processing, speaking and relinquishing turn ownership; they must not imply a source was checked, a form submitted or advice verified. Sensitive content should never be spoken aloud unexpectedly. The protected live interpreter contract remains separate.

### 4. Specific design test: one ordinary cancellation notice

Use a fictional, rights-cleared subscription message and a separately checked public cancellation policy. Compare the person's normal process against one static/clickable Flow prototype. Present (A) message only, (B) message plus verified policy, (C) stale/ambiguous policy. Ask the participant to identify **the actual cancellation route, any notice period, what is still unknown, and what has not been done yet**. Include a deliberately misleading “Cancel now” visual candidate to test whether button labelling causes false completion belief; reject it if it does.

Record: critical-condition comprehension; incorrect certainty; correct route selection; whether a participant wrongly believes cancellation occurred; taps/time; source-return success; unintended disclosure; interruptions and confidence *calibration*, not just subjective ease. Then repeat with a different everyday letter without teaching the interface again. Compare against ordinary browser/translation workflows. A pleasant prototype that creates false certainty **fails**.

### 5. Cross-project design knowledge worth transferring, without cloning applications

- **DAWT Media Gallery:** encounter before explanation; precise inspection; HOLD ≠ acceptance; leave/return without losing position. Transfer *control grammar*, not a gallery layout.
- **Transcribe:** keep original and interpretation distinguishable; source-linked passages and deliberate preservation, not automatic recording.
- **Quiet Reader:** readable source-first presentation, stable passage focus and re-entry.
- **Flow acoustic turn ownership:** clear, truthful readiness and control-return cues where spoken interaction is independently admitted.
- **Workroom/Workshop:** continuity of a deliberately retained undertaking; no obligation to turn ordinary one-off help into a project.

These are candidate donor principles, not proof that shared code or connectors are already integrated.

### 6. Decision requested of Flow owner

**Recommend ACCEPT WITH CLARIFICATIONS for Navigate as a bounded next-step explanation *inside* the locked selected-message encounter**, subject to owner reconciliation. Do **not** treat this recommendation as ratification. Keep “Understand” as the initial entry and **Speak · Understand · Respond** as the human-directed V1 lock until the owner explicitly resolves whether “Navigate” is public wording, an internal interaction intention, or later product evolution. Do not impose an additional tab or new launch dependency.

Preserve existing Flow EN↔PT runtime/holds and source permissions. No automatic site navigation, credential handling, form submission, cancellation, benefits determination, always-listening agent, universal institutional corpus, new model stack, or cross-project custody access is proposed. A real read-only official-source retrieval seam requires separate proof and authorisation.

**Keeper:** Flow succeeds when a person can recognise the truth of their situation, understand their options, and move forward independently—without mistaking helpful presentation for an action already taken.
